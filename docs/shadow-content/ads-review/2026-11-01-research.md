# What they'd tell you: paid acquisition research

Date: 2026-11-01
Scope: research only, for mylittleshadow.com/tell ($9.99 USD reading for a living pet). The Rainbow Bridge is out of scope and should not be advertised here.
Method: web search (standard and extended). Direct page fetches were blocked in this session for support.google.com, wordstream.com and similar hosts, so Google help pages are cited from search-result excerpts of those pages, not full reads. Google Trends, Google Keyword Planner, the Meta Ad Library and the Google Ads Transparency Center could not be reached. Anything not confirmed is marked "not verified". Every number below is a third-party estimate unless it says otherwise, with its source and date.

---

## Executive summary

1. No source gives search volume for "what would my dog say", "if my dog could talk" or "what is my dog thinking". Not verified either way; check Keyword Planner before bidding.
2. Those phrases are crowded by books ("If My Dog Could Talk" personalized book, "What Your Dog Is Thinking"), dozens of free or freemium "pet translator" apps, and TikTok AI-voice memes. Intent is mostly curiosity, entertainment and app-seeking, not "pay $9.99 on a website".
3. Gift and portrait terms show real commercial intent: "custom pet portrait" about 8,100 searches a month, CPC about $1.87; "pet portrait gift" CPC about $1.61 and strongly seasonal (590 in Nov 2025 vs 170 in Mar 2026) (RankHero estimates, 2026).
4. Benchmarks for Google Search in Animals & Pets: CPC about $3.97 (2025) to $4.06 (2026) per WordStream/LocaliQ reprints. At those prices $100 buys roughly 25 clicks, too few to learn much.
5. Meta Pets & Animals CPC benchmarks cluster around $0.76 to $1.12, CPM about $9.58 to $15 (vendor estimates, mixed dates). Several agency sources say novel, impulse, visual products fit Meta (demand creation) better than Google (demand capture).
6. Closest competitors are apps (PetWord, Pet Psychic, PawTalk, BarkSpeak, Zoomies AI) that lead with "what is your dog really thinking", give the first try free, sell $9.99 credit packs or subscriptions, add a share card, and carry "for entertainment" disclaimers.
7. Google policy: no source shows a ban on playful pet "voice" products, but the Unreliable claims policy bars claims that make an improbable result look likely, and pricing must clearly show what the user pays. Gmail ads restrict "psychic ability". Keep "mind reading", "translate", "psychic" out of ads.
8. Google's own RSA advice: many distinct headlines, keywords fully inside a headline, avoid pinning or pin several options. Ad Strength is a diagnostic and is not used in the auction (Google Ads Liaison, Apr 2024).
9. Money-back guarantee evidence is thin and mostly vendor anecdotes. The best academic hint: a generous guarantee raised believability for lesser-known sellers when the price felt low (Jeng et al. 2014). FTC guides say "money-back guarantee" means a full refund on request, conditions disclosed.
10. Before spending $100: set up purchase conversion tracking through Stripe Checkout (exclude checkout.stripe.com as a referral), decide the one question the $100 should answer, and expect it to buy a signal (click-through, chat starts), not statistically solid sales data.

## 3. Channel fit

- Agency and vendor sources broadly agree Google Search captures existing demand and Meta creates it, and that novel, impulse, visual products tend to fit Meta first, with Google added once people search for the brand or category. Examples: https://www.growthmentor.com/blog/google-ads-or-meta-first, https://echai.ventures/startingup/paid-acquisition/meta-or-google-ads-first-for-a-brand-nobody-is-searching-for-yet, https://www.iocea.com/insights/article/when-meta-ads-outperform-google-ads-and-why. These are opinion pieces, not measured studies.
- Google Search costs, Animals & Pets: average CPC $3.97, CTR 6.58%, conversion rate 13.07%, cost per lead $31.82 (WordStream/LocaliQ 2025 benchmarks, via https://localiq.com/blog/search-advertising-benchmarks/). 2026 edition: median CPC $4.06, CVR 16.22% (same publisher, reported in search excerpt). All-industry 2025 average CPC $5.26 (https://www.wordstream.com/blog/2025-google-ads-benchmarks). These "conversions" are mostly leads for pet services, not $9.99 impulse purchases, so the conversion rate is not transferable.
- Meta costs, Pets & Animals: CPC $1.12 and CPM $9.58 (Digital Applied, 2026, https://digitalapplied.com/blog/facebook-ads-benchmarks-2026-cpc-cpm-ctr-industry); CPC $0.76 on traffic campaigns (Megadigital, older, https://megadigital.ai/en/blog/facebook-ad-benchmarks/); pet products median CPC $1.10 (range $0.60 to $2.20), median CPM $15 (range $10 to $25), labelled medium confidence (https://arapovk-site.pages.dev/benchmarks/pet-products). Sources disagree; use as rough ranges.
- Simple arithmetic from those benchmarks (not a forecast): $100 at about $4 per Search click is about 25 clicks; at about $1 per Meta click it is about 100 clicks. With a $9.99 price, break-even per sale is $9.99 minus Stripe and AI costs.
- TikTok: surveys say short video drives impulse buys. Adobe: about 3 in 8 consumers bought after seeing a short video, 51% named TikTok as main impulse trigger (https://www.adobe.com/express/learn/blog/top-short-form-video-platforms). STYLESEAT survey: 55% of TikTok users made a social-driven impulse purchase (https://s3.amazonaws.com/media.mediapost.com/uploads/STYLESEAT_social_media_shopping.pdf). Vendor surveys, not peer reviewed. The AI dog-voice meme format already lives on TikTok (section 1).
- Pinterest: Pinterest's own data says gift searches every six seconds Oct to Dec, 69% open to new brands for gifting, holiday activity starts in September (https://business.pinterest.com/blog/holiday-2026-starts-now-a-pinterest-guide-to-success/, https://business.pinterest.com/en-au/blog/festive-gifting-pinterest/). Self-reported by Pinterest. Fits gift framing better than impulse self-purchase.
- Learning thresholds matter at $100: Meta ad sets are commonly said to need about 50 optimization events in 7 days to exit learning (many agency sources, e.g. https://admanage.ai/blog/facebook-ads-learning-phase-guide); one source says purchase campaigns need 10 per week since June 2024 (https://madgicx.com/answers/how-to-optimize-facebook-ads-for-conversions). Meta's own text: not verified. Google Target ROAS needs at least 15 conversions in 30 days (https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/13020501). A $100 test will not exit learning on purchase optimization on either platform.

Conclusion the evidence supports: Google Search is defensible as a cheap, controllable first test because it shows which queries convert, but the sources do not support it as the best channel for a novel $9.99 visual product. Meta/Instagram (and TikTok) match the product's shape: a visual, shareable output and a hook people didn't know to search for.

## 4. Ad policy

### Google Ads

- Unreliable claims: "Making inaccurate claims or claims that entice the user with an improbable result (even if this result is possible) as the likely outcome a user can expect is not allowed." Violations get a warning at least 7 days before suspension (https://support.google.com/adspolicy/answer/15936857). Risky phrasing: "know what your pet thinks", "translate", "hear your pet talk", "mind reading". Safer framing: playful, "in their voice", "built from what you tell us", which the landing page already uses.
- Pricing disclosure: "Failure to clearly and conspicuously disclose the payment model or full expense that a user will bear before and after purchase is not allowed" (Misrepresentation, https://support.google.com/adspolicy/answer/6020955). "Free" is allowed only if the offer is actually available as advertised (making offers that aren't actually available is misrepresentation). The chat is genuinely free, but an ad that says "free" without the $9.99 reading could read as a misleading impression of cost. The landing page shows "$9.99 USD" next to the Start button, which helps.
- Psychic and spiritual services: no Google Search policy page found that bans them; a third-party blog says Google handles them under Unreliable claims (https://www.graphed.com/blog/google-ads-for-psychics, not verified against Google). Gmail ads list "spiritual abilities and guidance, such as mysticism and psychic ability" as restricted (https://support.google.com/adspolicy/answer/6376747). LinkedIn bans fortune telling except "amusement" (https://conductatlas.com/platform/linkedin/linkedin-advertising-policies/provision/CA-P-063672/occult-pursuits-advertising-restricted/). An "entertainment purposes" exemption on Google: not verified. Best practice: never use psychic, medium, communicator or afterlife language for /tell.
- AI claims: no Google Ads rule found requiring an AI disclosure for this kind of product (only election ads have one). Not verified beyond that.
- Guarantee claims: no Google-specific guarantee rule found. US law applies: FTC Guides 16 CFR 239 say "Satisfaction Guarantee" or "Money Back Guarantee" should be used only if the seller refunds the full purchase price on request, and material conditions must be disclosed (https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-16/chapter-I/subchapter-B/part-239). The /tell terms (full refund by email within 30 days) fit that, provided the ad does not imply anything broader.
- Trademarks: Google does not restrict trademarks as keywords; it may restrict them in ad text after a valid owner complaint, and restricts confusing use by competitors (https://support.google.com/adspolicy/answer/6118). "If My Dog Could Talk" is a commercial book title (I See Me); trademark status not verified. Using such phrases descriptively as keywords is low risk; avoid naming competitor brands or apps in ad text.
- Destination: ads can be disapproved for destinations with insufficient original content (https://support.google.com/adspolicy/answer/16427718).

### Meta

- Personal attributes: ads must not assert or imply personal attributes of the viewer (https://transparency.meta.com/policies/ad-standards/objectionable-content/personal-attributes). Mainly about age, health, religion, finances; a line like "Your dog thinks you're..." targets the pet, not the viewer, so it is likely fine, but avoid "You're lonely" or grief-adjacent lines entirely.
- Unrealistic outcomes: rule covers health, weight loss, economic opportunity (https://transparency.meta.com/policies/ad-standards/deceptive-content/unrealistic-outcomes). Not a direct fit, but deceptive-content review would still catch "real translation" claims.
- AI labels: Meta automatically labels ads made or edited with some generative AI tools ("AI info"), detected via C2PA metadata (https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/meta-adds-updated-disclosure-tags-for-ai-generated-ads/824658, https://marketingdive.com/news/sociable-meta-adds-updated-disclosure-tags-for-ai-generated-ads/824833). A claim that undisclosed AI is 14% of rejections (Storrito) is not verified.
- Psychic/fortune-telling specific Meta rule: not verified.

## 5. Responsive Search Ads: what Google says

From Google Ads Help excerpts (pages could not be fetched in full):

- An RSA can hold up to 15 headlines and 4 descriptions (https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/7684791). Google recommends writing many distinct headlines and avoiding repeated words or ideas (https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/9023565).
- Keywords: for Ad Strength, a keyword must sit entirely in one headline, not split across two; keywords too long for a headline can go in a description (https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/9921843).
- Pinning: Google advises against pinning for most advertisers because it limits combinations; if you must pin, pin several versions to the same position (https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/7684791, https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/9921843).
- Ad Strength: measures relevance, quantity and diversity of assets against the ad group's keywords; it does not measure landing page quality (https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/9921843). It "is not used in the auction" (Ginny Marvin, Google Ads Liaison, April 2024, https://seroundtable.com/google-ad-strength-auction-37190.html). Google's Search ads guide credits a 12% conversion lift to moving from Poor to Excellent; Marvin earlier cited 9% (https://www.searchenginejournal.com/all-your-ad-strength-questions-answered/514092/). Commentators note Average-rated ads can outperform Excellent ones (https://www.relevantaudience.com/google-ads-en/google-ads-ad-strength/).
- Price and offers: Google provides price assets (at least 3 items, 5 or more recommended, 25-character headers) and promotion assets (money or percent off, optional occasion label) (https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/7065415, https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/7367521). With one product at one price, a price asset needing 3 items is a poor fit. Google's help excerpts found do not say whether putting a price in a headline helps or hurts; not verified. Policy (section 4) does require the payment model to be clear.
- Landing page experience is part of Quality Score, defined as how relevant and useful the page is to people who click. Google's advice: closely match the ad and keyword, repeat the ad's call to action on the page, make the action quick and easy, put key information near the top, avoid clutter and pop-ups, provide useful original information such as customer reviews, design for mobile (https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/2404197, https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/6238826).
- Relevance to /tell: the page headline is "What they'd tell you / Let them finally get a word in". A searcher typing "what would my dog say" sees no matching words above the fold. The site has separate pages at /what-would-my-dog-say and /what-is-my-cat-thinking that may match those keywords better as final URLs (not reviewed here).

## 6. Guarantee messaging

Evidence for leading with a money-back guarantee:
- Jeng et al. (2014, Service Science): when perceived price was low, a more generous guarantee raised believability for both well-known and lesser-known stores; when price was high it only helped well-known stores (https://ideas.repec.org/a/inm/orserv/v6y2014i3p179-189.html). A low price plus an unknown seller is the /tell case.
- Signaling theory (Moorthy and Srinivasan 1995, Marketing Science): guarantees are a credible quality signal because they cost weak sellers more (https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/fpi/10.1287/mksc.14.4.442).
- Vendor tests: a trust-element test for Movavi found security icon plus guarantee gave a 5.5% lift, guarantee not isolated (2Checkout, https://blog.2checkout.com/?p=15309); a visible 30-day guarantee raised sales 21% on a $197 digital product (Quick Sprout, https://www.quicksprout.com/what-converts-better-free-trial-versus-money-back-guarantee/). Anecdotal, different price points.

Evidence against or for caution:
- Desmet (2014, Journal of Business Research): the effect of a money-back guarantee was small and moderated by other quality cues (https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/jbrese/v67y2014i9p1971-1978.html).
- Behe and Fry (2020, Journal of Risk Research, plants): a 30-day guarantee reduced perceived risk for $5 to $10 items, but the effect shrank above $20; overall effect per day of guarantee was small (https://ideas.repec.org/a/taf/jriskr/v23y2020i2p242-258.html). Low-price items have low risk to start with.
- No study found on guarantees in ad copy (versus on the landing page) for low-price impulse buys. Not verified.
- A guarantee in an ad uses space that could carry the hook. With $9.99 at stake, the main barrier is likely "is this worth it / is it any good", which a sample reading may answer better than a refund promise (inference, not sourced).

How brands phrase it:
- Crown & Paw: "100% Satisfaction Guarantee", delivered as unlimited previews and revisions rather than refunds (https://resources.pangovet.com/pet-lifestyle/general/crown-paw-pet-portraits-review).
- FTC: the claim "Money Back Guarantee" means full refund on request; conditions (e.g. "within 30 days") should be part of the claim, not hidden (https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-16/chapter-I/subchapter-B/part-239).
- 30 days is the most common window and beat 37 days in one split test (https://dfoglobal.atlassian.net/wiki/x/toDzC, vendor wiki, not verified).

Note for the owner's preference: the current draft ads include "Not them? Try again free" as a headline and description, and the landing page footer promise also leads with "try again free". The owner wants that as a fallback only; the ads team should be aware.

## 7. Other things to know before the first $100

1. Purchase tracking through Stripe Checkout: Stripe says to add checkout.stripe.com to GA4's referral exclusion list, or the return from Stripe can steal credit from the ad (https://docs.stripe.com/payments/checkout/analyze-conversion-funnel). Cross-domain `_gl` linking does not survive the Stripe-hosted page (https://docs.analyzify.com/referral-exclusions-on-ga4). Set up a Google Ads conversion (or GA4 key event imported to Ads) on the paid return before spending, plus a secondary "chat started" or "reached [[READY]]" event so a $100 test has something to count.
2. $100 cannot exit platform learning on purchases (section 3). Decide what it should answer: for example, which hook gets the cheapest chat starts, or whether any search query converts at all.
3. Zero proof: competitors lead with review counts (Crown & Paw "34000+ 5-Star Reviews"). Google lists customer reviews as useful original landing page content (https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/6238826). Getting even a few real readings (friends, family) to show as samples, with permission, would fill this.
4. Seasonality: gift searches and Pinterest gift activity peak October to December (sections 1 and 3). The launch timing is good for a gift angle; January will be cheaper but colder.
5. Competing with free: many apps give a free first reading (Pet Psychic, PetWord). The /tell chat is free but the reading is not; ads promising "free" may attract people who leave at the paywall.
6. Negative keywords: the draft already blocks translator, psychic, grief terms and book/song. Note that the draft also targets "pet talk app", "talking pet app", "dog voice app"; people searching "app" likely want a mobile app (section 2). Judgement call for the ads team.
7. Canada: Canadian Competition Act rules on price and guarantee claims were not researched here; not verified. Pricing in USD should stay explicit for Canadian visitors (the page already says "USD").
8. Unverified data to fetch manually before bidding: Keyword Planner volumes and suggested bids for the section 1 phrases in US and CA; Google Trends comparison of "if my dog could talk", "what would my dog say", "custom pet portrait"; Meta Ad Library search for active pet-voice app ads.

## Recommendations the evidence supports

1. Treat the first $100 as a learning test, not a sales channel. Before it starts, track purchases (with checkout.stripe.com excluded as a referrer) and a free-chat milestone event.
2. If Google Search goes first, expect roughly 25 clicks at the 2025 to 2026 Animals & Pets average CPC. Use phrase or exact match on a short list, and judge it on search terms and chat starts, not on sales.
3. Plan Meta/Instagram as the likely main channel for this product's shape (novel, visual, shareable, impulse). Use the share card and real sample readings as the creative. Test Meta in parallel or next if the budget allows.
4. Keep ad language on the safe side of Google's Unreliable claims policy: playful voice, built from what you share, for fun. Never translation, mind reading, psychic or "what they really think" stated as fact.
5. Whenever an ad says the chat is free, also make it clear the reading costs $9.99, to meet Google's payment-model disclosure rule and to avoid paywall bounce.
6. A money-back guarantee is reasonable as a secondary trust line (low price plus unknown seller is where the research shows it helps believability). Weak evidence supports leading with it. If used, phrase it as a real refund with its condition ("money back within 30 days"), per the FTC guides.
7. Give Google many distinct, unpinned headlines with each target keyword fully inside at least one headline. Treat Ad Strength as a checklist, not a goal.
8. Point keyword groups at the most matching page (the site's /what-would-my-dog-say and /what-is-my-cat-thinking pages may suit "say/thinking" keywords better than /tell), as long as they carry a clear path to start.
9. Use the gift angle in November and December, but only claim what is true: a digital card, made by someone who knows the pet.
10. Before bidding, collect the missing numbers yourself (Keyword Planner, Trends, Meta Ad Library). This report could not verify them.
