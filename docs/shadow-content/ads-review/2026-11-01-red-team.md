# Red team: "What they'd tell you" Google Ads draft and funnel

Date: 2026-11-01
Reviewed: `docs/shadow-content/2026-11-01-ads-and-directories.md` (the draft), `public/shadow/tell.html` (landing page), `public/shadow/index.html` (home, sitelink target), `public/shadow/terms.html`, `public/shadow/contact.html`, `public/shadow/privacy.html`, `docs/shadow-content/2026-10-31-what-theyd-tell-you-prompts.md` (samples), `docs/shadow-content/2026-10-31-launch-posts.md` (social).
Mode: diagnosis only. No rewrites. Line numbers refer to the files as of today.

Short version: the ad describes the plumbing (host, microphone, chat, card, refund) instead of the payoff (a funny line your dog would actually say), it is aimed at curiosity and gift-shopping searches that do not buy a $9.99 digital text, it bids blind, and it lands on a page that shows nothing until the visitor has typed for several minutes. The product output (Waffles, Duchess, Clementine) is genuinely good. The campaign hides it.

---

## 1. The scroller

A dog owner types "what would my dog say" and sees four other results: probably a YouTube video, a "dog translator" app, a listicle, maybe a Chewy or BarkBox ad. This ad has one line of grey URL, two or three headlines and one or two descriptions to win the click.

### What each headline actually communicates (ad group 1, draft lines 55 to 69)

| # | Headline | What a stranger reads | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | What would your dog say? | Echoes the search. Fine, but it is a question, not an offer. | OK alone, weak as the only hook |
| 2 | What would your cat say? | Shown to dog searchers too, because dog and cat keywords share one ad group. | Mismatch risk |
| 3 | If your pet could talk | Sounds like an article title or a meme. | Vague |
| 4 | Give your pet the microphone | Internal metaphor. Out of context it reads as a pet karaoke toy or a recording gadget. | Jargon |
| 5 | They finally get a word in | Who is "they"? No noun anywhere. Means nothing to someone who has not seen the landing page. | Meaningless out of context |
| 6 | A funny reading for your pet | "Reading" reads as tarot, psychic or "reading aloud to your pet". The one word the brand is trying to avoid (psychic) is the word it chose. | Ambiguous, and wrong associations |
| 7 | Their take, in their voice | Again no noun. "Their" whose? Could be anyone. | Vague |
| 8 | Built from what you tell us | Mechanics, and slightly defensive: pre-admits that the output is just your own input. | Defensive |
| 9 | Type or talk, add a photo | Input method. Nobody chooses an ad because of its input method. | Filler |
| 10 | Chat free, reading $9.99 | Price is useful as a filter. "Chat free" makes it sound like a chatbot, and invites free-seekers the campaign then blocks with the negative "free". | Mixed signal |
| 11 | Not them? Try again free | Violates the owner's new rule. Also defensive: the ad is apologising for failure before the reader knows what the product is. | Must go |
| 12 | Money-back promise | Pure risk language. From an unknown brand at $9.99 it reads as "this might not work". | Defensive |
| 13 | Plus a card made for sharing | Starts with "Plus", so it only works after another headline that names the main thing. RSAs do not guarantee that order. | Fragment |
| 14 | For dogs, cats and more | Filler. | Filler |
| 15 | What they'd tell you | Product name with no noun. To a stranger it is a sentence fragment. | Meaningless out of context |

Count: of 15 headlines, only 1 and 2 say anything a searcher instantly understands, and only 10 says what you get for money. There is no headline that shows a single funny line (the actual product), none that says "AI", none that says "letter" or "message from your dog", and no call to action ("Try it", "Start", "Get yours").

### Bad combinations that Google will happily serve

RSAs pick 2 or 3 headlines at random-ish. The draft claims (line 15) "Every headline below is written to stand on its own in any order." That is false. Real combos from this pool:

- "They finally get a word in | What they'd tell you | Their take, in their voice". Three pronouns, zero nouns. You cannot tell it is about pets.
- "Not them? Try again free | Money-back promise | Chat free, reading $9.99". The entire ad is about refunds and price. No product.
- "Give your pet the microphone | Type or talk, add a photo". Reads like a voice-recording app.
- "What would your cat say? | For dogs, cats and more" shown on "what would my dog say". Wrong animal, then a shrug.
- "A funny reading for your pet | Built from what you tell us". Sounds like a horoscope generator that admits it is making it up.
- "Plus a card made for sharing | Money-back promise". Starts mid-thought, ends on a disclaimer.
- Ad group 2: "Small crimes, fully explained | Send it to the group chat". Reads like true crime or a legal service.

### Descriptions (lines 73 to 76)

- D1: "Tell a friendly host about your pet, then they get the mic". "They" grammatically points at the host, not the pet. 89 of 90 characters spent on process.
- D2: "Not mind reading, just them with attitude." Negation plants the idea it was going to be mind reading. Nobody searching "what would my dog say" was worried about that.
- D3: Fine as a price line; "comes with a card made for sharing" is the only benefit, and it is the minor one.
- D4: Free retry plus refund. Banned line, and the third defensive message in a 4-description ad.

### Ad group 2 headlines (lines 99 to 113)

- The word "gift" appears in zero of 15 headlines, in an ad group whose keywords are mostly "gift for dog owner". Ad relevance and Quality Score will be poor, and the searcher sees no answer to their query.
- "Personality test" keywords are answered with "Funny pet personality reading". People searching "dog personality test" want a quiz with a result type, usually free.
- "Make one for a friend's pet" undercuts itself: the host asks for three words, a habit, a routine and questions. Most gift-givers cannot answer those about someone else's pet.
- "Ready in a few minutes" is the closest thing to a benefit, and is generous: it ignores the chat time and Stripe.

Would they click? A curious dog owner might click headline 1 out of idle curiosity (the worst kind of paid click, see section 2). A buyer would not, because the ad never shows what the thing is or why it is funny.

## 2. The performance marketer

### Keywords and intent (lines 41 to 51, 85 to 95)

- "what is my dog thinking", "what is my cat thinking": overwhelmingly behaviour and body-language research ("why does my dog stare at me"). Informational, zero purchase intent. Your own SEO article `/what-is-my-cat-thinking` is titled "A translation guide", which tells you what that searcher wants.
- "if my dog could talk", "if my cat could talk": book titles, songs, poems, YouTube compilations, memes. The draft adds "book, song, lyrics, movie" as negatives, but most of this traffic does not type those words. It types the phrase and clicks the first thing.
- "what would my dog say", "what would my pet say": curiosity and meme intent (TikTok voiceover trend, "dog voice" filters). Some fit, but very few expect to pay.
- "pet talk app", "talking pet app": this is Talking Tom and similar games, very often kids. Terms say buyers must be 18 or older (`terms.html`, "Who can buy"). This keyword recruits minors and app-store seekers; the product is not an app.
- "dog voice app": people wanting a voice changer or text-to-speech for their own videos.
- "gift for dog owner", "gift for cat owner", "unique gift for pet owner", "funny gift for dog lover": high-competition, Shopping-dominated, physical-product intent, and in November the most expensive pet terms of the year. A $9.99 digital text you cannot gift-wrap and cannot fill in without knowing the pet intimately. The draft itself admits (line 125, line 305) gift searchers may click and leave, then recommends running it anyway as an option.
- "custom pet card", "funny pet card": greeting-card and printed-card intent. Your negatives block "print" and "printed", but the keyword itself is physical.
- "pet personality test" and friends: free quiz intent.
- Missing: the phrases that are closest to the product and might actually convert, for example "letter from my dog", "message from my dog", "my dog's personality in words", "funny things my dog would say", "dog roast", "what my cat thinks of me". Not tested, but they are closer to the real output than "gift for dog owner".

### Match types

All phrase match, 22 keywords, two ad groups, no exact match control. With Maximize clicks and no conversion data, phrase match on broad curiosity phrases will spend on the cheapest, longest-tail, least-intent variants Google can find. There is no plan to start tight (exact) and expand.

### Ad group structure

- Dog and cat keywords in one ad group with one RSA containing both "What would your dog say?" and "What would your cat say?". Google will show the cat headline to dog searches. Split by animal at minimum.
- Ad group 2 mixes three unrelated intents (gift, personality test, card) under one ad. Each needs a different promise and none of them has it.

### Negatives (lines 131 to 175)

Over-blocking:
- "toy" kills "what would my toy poodle say" and every toy breed (Yorkie owners write "toy" a lot).
- "translator" and "translate" block the single largest adjacent intent ("dog translator"). Many of those searchers know there is no real translator and want exactly a funny version. That is a judgment call, but the draft blocks it without testing it, while bidding on far worse intent ("gift for dog owner").
- "free" blocks people who would happily do the free chat and then pay, while the ad itself says "Chat free". Pick one stance.
- "medium" blocks "medium sized dog" searches (minor), but it is there for a real reason, see section 5.
- "passed", "loss": fine for safety, low cost of over-blocking.

Missing:
- Kids and games: "game", "games", "kids", "talking tom", "play store", "app store", "download", "apk".
- Meme and content: "meme", "memes", "tiktok", "filter", "voice changer", "voiceover", "video", "youtube", "funny videos", "reddit".
- Free-tool hunters you cannot serve: "quiz" (for the personality-test keywords), "generator" (decide deliberately), "chatgpt".
- Behaviour research: "why does", "body language", "signs", "how to tell", "meaning", "science", "study".
- Physical gifts: "ornament", "blanket", "pillow", "socks", "amazon", "etsy", "personalized" (decide), "frame", "portrait".
- The draft negates "rainbow bridge" as phrase but not "in memory", "remembrance", "pet loss", "after death"; the single words cover most, fine.

### Bidding, tracking and budget (lines 7, 8, 307)

- Maximize clicks with no conversion tracking. The draft lists conversion tracking as an open "decision" (line 307). It is not optional. Maximize clicks optimises for the cheapest clicks, which on these keywords means the least qualified people. You will learn nothing about which keyword sells.
- The page already fires a GA4 `purchase` event (`tell.html` line 479) and `begin_checkout` (line 460), but only if the buyer returns to the same tab from Stripe with `?paid=`. Not imported into Google Ads, no Google Ads conversion tag, no enhanced conversions.
- Stripe hosted checkout is an off-site redirect. Unless `checkout.stripe.com` is added to GA4's unwanted referrals, returning buyers start a new session attributed to stripe.com, and the purchase will not be credited to google/cpc at all.
- No mention of enabling auto-tagging (gclid). Manual UTMs alone do not let Google Ads see conversions.
- Budget and max CPC left as "[Brew decides]" with no guidance on unit economics. This is the core of the problem (section 6): revenue per sale is about $9.99 minus Stripe fees and model and voice costs, call it roughly $9. With a cold, multi-step funnel, a 1 to 3 percent click-to-purchase rate would be optimistic for launch. That puts break-even CPC around $0.09 to $0.27. Those are estimates, not data, but the draft gives Brew no way to see that he is probably buying at a loss from click one.

### Geo, language, schedule

- US and Canada, prices in USD only. Canadians see a USD price with no CAD hint; minor, but a conversion drag and a source of "surprise" bank charges.
- No French and no Quebec exclusion while the page is English only; minor.
- All hours, all devices. With voice autoplay on by default (see section 4), late-night and at-work mobile clicks get a talking web page. Fine to leave on, but watch it.

### Sitelinks (lines 195 to 230)

- "See sample lines" goes to the home page. The home page sells three "coming soon" products and The Rainbow Bridge ("a gentle medium helps you hear from them", `index.html` line 298). Paid traffic for a joke product is sent to a page whose finale is a grief and medium product. The draft notices this (line 235) and leaves it as a decision.
- "Money-back promise" sitelink: a whole sitelink slot spent on a refund policy, with the banned free-retry line as its first description (line 212), landing on Terms.
- "Privacy": nobody clicks a pet ad's privacy sitelink. Wasted slot.
- Missing: an actual sample reading page (does not exist; the draft notes this at line 234 and moves on). That is the single most useful sitelink you could have.
- The existing SEO pages `/what-would-my-dog-say` and `/what-is-my-cat-thinking` are not used as sitelinks or as keyword-matched final URLs.

### Display path and final URL

- Display path "tell/your-pet" is fine but bland. "dog-letter" or "your-dog-says" would echo intent.
- One final URL for every keyword. "what would my dog say" and "gift for dog owner" land on the same hero ("Let them finally get a word in."). No message match per intent.

## 3. The skeptic and buyer

Trust gaps, in the order a buyer feels them:

1. **Unknown brand, launched yesterday.** No reviews, no examples from real pets, no press, no social proof. The draft forbids testimonials (correctly, there are none) but replaces proof with nothing.
2. **"What is it, exactly?"** Not shown anywhere before payment. On `/tell` there is no sample reading, no sample card, no image at all. The home page has sample one-liners; the landing page has none. The value is described (host, mic, card), never demonstrated.
3. **$9.99 for text.** The buyer's mental comparison is a free ChatGPT prompt ("write a funny letter from my beagle who howls at the microwave"). Nothing in the ad or page says why this is better: the interview that pulls out specifics, the voice narration, the card. The "Hear it out loud" narration (a real differentiator, `tell.html` lines 525 to 534) is not mentioned in any ad.
4. **AI is hidden.** The ads never say AI. The `/tell` hero never says AI; only the footer does ("An AI experience for fun and celebration", line 264). The host prompt says "Never mention being an AI unless sincerely asked" (prompts doc line 30). A buyer who thought a person wrote it is a refund and a complaint. The directory listing is the only place that says "AI" plainly (line 256, line 280).
5. **Defensive framing undercuts the pitch.** "Not mind reading", "Built from what you tell us", "Money-back promise", "Not them? Try again free" all answer objections the buyer did not have yet, and in doing so create them: is it a gimmick, does it usually miss, why do they expect refunds?
6. **The opposite problem on the home page.** The sitelink target overclaims: "sounds exactly like them" (`index.html` line 88), "a little too accurate" (line 142), "uncannily specific" (line 184). Ad says "not mind reading", destination says "exactly like them". The buyer gets both messages within one click.
7. **"Reading"** is a psychic-industry word, used on a site whose other product is a medium. A skeptic connects those dots.
8. **Not answered anywhere above the fold:** How long does it take? What exactly do I get (word count, card, audio)? Can I see one? Is it mine to keep, and can I get it back later? Do I need an account? Is my photo kept? (Answered only in the footer.)

## 4. The landing page after the click

### Message match

- Ad: "What would your dog say?" Page H1: "Let them finally get a word in." (`tell.html` line 231). Kicker: "What they'd tell you". Neither echoes the query, neither says dog, cat or pet. The pronoun problem from the ads continues on the page.
- Gift ad group lands on the same page, which contains no mention of gifts at all.

### Time to value

Steps between landing and seeing anything good:
1. Read hero, click Start.
2. Host types out its first message character by character (14 ms per character, line 290) after an API round trip, and speaks it aloud.
3. Answer: name and kind of animal.
4. Answer: three words.
5. Answer: a habit.
6. Answer: a routine.
7. Answer: up to three questions.
8. Optional photo (another host turn).
9. Click "Hear from them, $9.99".
10. Leave the site for Stripe, pay, return.
11. Wait for the reading to generate.

That is roughly 5 to 7 model round trips plus typing before the first funny line, and the first funny line is behind the paywall. Nothing on the page earns the visitor's patience before step 1.

### Price placement

- The price is disclosed under the Start button in small grey text (line 236). Disclosure is fine. The problem is psychological: the ad shouts "Chat free", the visitor invests several minutes, and the price becomes a wall at the end. People who came for "free" (which the ad invited) feel baited. Expect high abandonment at "Hear from them, $9.99" and some angry feedback.

### Friction before checkout

- Voice is on by default (`voiceOn = true`, line 316). Clicking Start unlocks audio and the host speaks aloud. A phone user at work or in bed gets a talking page with no warning. The toggle only appears after the first reply.
- Redirect to hosted Stripe, then back. In-app browsers (Instagram, Gmail, Facebook) and some mobile browsers can drop `sessionStorage` across that hop; the code handles it by restarting the interview "already paid" (line 476), which means the buyer has to answer everything again after paying.

### After checkout: the reading can be lost

- After return, `history.replaceState` strips `?paid=` (line 472) and the session id lives only in memory. The saved state (line 459) does not include it. Refresh the page, or the phone reloads the tab in the background, and the paid reading is gone from the user's view. There is no email, account or permalink.
- "Start another pet" calls `location.reload()` (line 545) with no warning, which also discards the reading if the card was not saved.
- Privacy page line 56 says the reading is kept 60 days "That way you can reload it". The UI gives no way to do that. A promise the product cannot keep, on a paid item, is a refund and chargeback generator.

### Above the fold

- No sample reading, no sample card, no image. The only visual on the page is the nav logo. The OG image is a dusk sunset scene, not a reading.
- On a 375 px phone: nav, kicker, a 3 to 4 line H1 at about 38 px, a 5 line lede, Start, then the price line. The "steps" sit below the fold. A visitor sees text about a microphone and a button.

### Off-message links on a paid landing page

- Footer links The Rainbow Bridge (line 266). Paid traffic is one tap from the grief and medium product.
- Footer says "try again free from your reading" (line 265). See section 5.

### The refund path does not match the ad

- Ads promise "get your money back". On the page, the guarantee UI only offers a free retry button (line 588). The refund is email only. The API already returns `canRefund` (`src/app/api/bridge/route.ts` line 174) but the UI never uses it. "Money-back" in an ad that resolves to "write us an email" is weaker than it sounds.

## 5. Policy and risk

### Google Ads

- **Psychic and medium adjacency.** The domain's home page (a sitelink destination) offers "a gentle medium helps you hear from them" (`index.html` line 298) and a $14.99 Bridge reading. Third-party guides report Google treats psychic services under its Unreliable claims policy and that automated review flags psychic terms ([Unreliable claims](https://support.google.com/adspolicy/answer/15936857), [graphed.com summary](https://www.graphed.com/blog/google-ads-for-psychics); I could not fetch Google's page from this session to quote it, so verify). Combined with the word "reading" in headlines and descriptions, a manual reviewer could reasonably class the account as psychic services. Low probability of outright disapproval, real probability of "Limited" status or extra review. The bigger risk is brand, not policy.
- **Guarantee claims.** Google requires a clear, accessible refund policy if you guarantee results ([False, misleading, or unrealistic claims](https://support.google.com/adspolicy/answer/6086777)). Terms has one, but its first remedy is the retry and the refund is email only; ad copy says "money back" flatly. Keep the ad's promise exactly as narrow as the Terms.
- **"Free" with a required payment.** The ad says "Chat free" for a product whose only deliverable is paid. Google tightened rules on free claims and hidden charges from October 2025 (reported by [ppcnewsfeed](https://ppcnewsfeed.com/ppc-news/2025-09/google-tightens-rules-on-hidden-fees-free-trials/) and [Kliken](https://help.kliken.com/en/articles/12401029-google-ads-policy-update-dishonest-pricing-practices-effective-october-2025); third-party, verify). "Chat free, reading $9.99" discloses the price, so it is probably fine, but "Not them? Try again free" alone in an ad, without the price, is the kind of fragment that reads as a free offer.
- **Business transparency.** Contact page is an email only, no business name, address or phone (`contact.html`). New advertisers with thin business info get more Misrepresentation scrutiny. Worth fixing before spending.
- **Minors.** "talking pet app" and "pet talk app" draw children; Terms require 18+.
- **Structured data.** `tell.html` Product schema declares `MerchantReturnPolicy` with `applicableCountry: "CA"` only and `returnFees: FreeReturn` for a digital item (lines 205 to 210). Not an Ads policy issue, but inaccurate markup on the ad's landing page.

### Meta (launch posts, section 3)

- Low policy risk. The Meta ad variants make no personal-attribute or medical claims. "Not them? Money back." (launch posts line 105) is fine. Sample pets are labelled. The risk is the same destination problem: Meta reviews the landing domain, and the domain sells a medium.

### Complaint and chargeback risk

1. Reading lost on refresh or "Start another pet" after paying (section 4). Most likely chargeback source.
2. Ad and page imply "money back"; actual path is rate under 3 stars, write a reason, get offered a retry, then email.
3. Free-chat expectation meeting a paywall after several minutes.
4. AI not disclosed in the ad or hero.
5. Gift buyers who cannot answer the questions about someone else's pet, paid anyway, get a generic reading.
6. USD charges to Canadian cards with FX fees.

## 6. Strategy

### Is Google Search the right first channel?

No. Search harvests existing demand. Nobody is searching for "funny AI reading in my pet's voice for $9.99"; the nearest searches are curiosity, memes, behaviour research and physical gifts. This is an impulse, show-me, share-it product: the purchase trigger is seeing a hilarious line about a dog like yours, which is a visual, social, feed-native moment. That points to:
- Friends and own network first, to get 20 to 50 real readings and permissioned real examples (the friend-text in launch posts line 33 is the best thing in either draft).
- Short video and Meta/Instagram/TikTok with the actual card and actual lines as the creative.
- The built-in share loop (the card carries `mylittleshadow.com`, the share text links to `/tell`).
- Search later, small, exact match, only on the handful of phrases that convert once tracking proves it.

### The single biggest reason this campaign would fail

Unit economics with a blind, cold, high-friction funnel. A $9.99 one-time digital purchase leaves about $9 to pay for clicks. Bidding Maximize clicks on curiosity and gift keywords, with no conversion tracking, sending people to a page that shows nothing and asks for several minutes of typing before a paywall, will plausibly convert at around 1 percent. That needs clicks under about 10 cents to break even. Every other problem in this review makes the conversion rate worse; this one means even a perfect ad would lose money unless the funnel shows the product first and the campaign can see what sells.

## Top 10 problems (most damaging first)

1. **Critical. The ads never show the product.** Evidence: headlines "Give your pet the microphone", "They finally get a word in", "Type or talk, add a photo", "What they'd tell you"; no headline or description contains a single line in a pet's voice, while the samples ("The microwave beeps, I answer. That's just good manners.", "I am small, but the thump is not.") are excellent. Fix: lead with the payoff and real-sounding example lines, plain nouns (dog, cat, message, letter), a call to action. Drop internal metaphors (mic, microphone, get a word in) and pronoun-only headlines.

2. **Critical. Blind bidding on a break-even-impossible funnel.** Evidence: "Bidding: Maximize clicks", budget and CPC "[Brew decides]", conversion tracking listed as an optional decision (line 307). Fix: Google Ads conversion on purchase (imported GA4 `purchase` or Ads tag), auto-tagging, `checkout.stripe.com` as unwanted referral, a stated break-even CPC from price and expected conversion rate, a small test budget with a kill rule.

3. **Critical. Keywords target curiosity, research, kids and physical gifts, not buyers.** Evidence: "what is my dog thinking", "if my dog could talk", "talking pet app", "dog voice app", "gift for dog owner", "custom pet card", "dog personality test". Fix: cut the gift ad group at launch, cut thinking, app and test keywords, split dog and cat, start exact match on the few closest phrases, rebuild negatives (add games, kids, meme, quiz, behaviour terms; reconsider "toy", "translator", "free").

4. **Critical. The landing page shows nothing before asking for minutes of typing, then a paywall.** Evidence: `tell.html` hero has no sample, card or image; value arrives after 5 or more chat turns and Stripe. Fix: a real sample reading and card above the fold, a hero that echoes the query ("What would your dog say?"), clear what-you-get and how-long, keyword-matched landing variants.

5. **Critical. Free retry promoted everywhere, against the owner's rule.** Evidence: "Not them? Try again free" (draft lines 65, 110), descriptions lines 76 and 120, sitelink line 212, directory lines 256, 270, 280, posts lines 25, 57, 119, live `tell.html` line 265 and `contact.html` line 54. Fix: remove every instance listed in section 5 from ads, posts, listings and public page copy; the retry lives only in the post-reading under-3-stars flow and the Terms mechanism.

6. **Major. Defensive and contradictory trust messaging.** Evidence: "Not mind reading, just them with attitude", "Built from what you tell us", "Money-back promise" in a third of the slots; while the sitelinked home page says "sounds exactly like them", "a little too accurate", "uncannily specific". Fix: one honest, confident positioning (funny, built from your stories) used everywhere; at most one risk-reversal line; remove the overclaims or the disclaimers so they stop contradicting each other.

7. **Major. Paid traffic routed to the medium and grief product.** Evidence: "See sample lines" sitelink to the home page, which ends "a gentle medium helps you hear from them" (`index.html` line 298); `/tell` footer links The Rainbow Bridge (line 266); "reading" in headlines. Fix: no paid link to the home page; a dedicated sample page; remove the Bridge link from the paid landing variant; stop calling it a "reading" in ads.

8. **Major. Paid readings can vanish, and Privacy says they can be reloaded.** Evidence: `history.replaceState` drops `?paid=` (line 472), saved state omits the session id (line 459), "Start another pet" reloads (line 545); Privacy line 56 "you can reload it".
